Swarm Lab

Dozens of identical tiny bots share one small neural-net brain. Each bot sees only its own surroundings, yet the swarm moves heavy loads, builds relay chains and forms rings. Pick a job, then watch the swarm do it or teach it from scratch.

Shared policy

One brain, many bots

Every bot runs the same 14-neuron network on its own local view. Nobody is in charge and nobody sees the whole picture.

Neuroevolution

How it was taught

An evolution strategy tries many small random changes to the brain, keeps what scored better, and repeats. The trained brains here took 30 to 150 rounds.

Teamwork by design

Why one bot is not enough

The block will not move under fewer than about five aligned pushes. The towers are out of radio range of each other. A ring needs spacing between bots.

Limits

Honest notes

Trained at 20 bots. Other sizes work to a degree: Bridge needs around 16 or more bots to reach across. This is a flat 2D toy, not a physics-accurate robot model.
